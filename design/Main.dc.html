Skyflow · PII Detection Benchmark

PII Detection Accuracy & Recall

How Skyflow compares to the OpenAI Privacy Filter and Microsoft Presidio — two of the most widely used open-source PII detectors — at finding personally identifiable information across multilingual text, benchmarked on the ai4privacy PII-Masking-300k dataset.

Overview

This overview summarizes how Skyflow detects personally identifiable information (PII) across multilingual text, and how its accuracy compares to two of the most widely used open-source PII detectors — the OpenAI Privacy Filter and Microsoft Presidio. All three were run over the same 1,000-example benchmark and scored the same way, so the numbers are directly comparable.

For most teams the metric that matters most is recall — the share of real PII the system actually catches. A missed identifier is a compliance and security risk, so the results below lead with recall, where Skyflow’s advantage is largest.

How accuracy is measured

Results use three standard measures, computed at the span level so comparisons stay consistent across detectors:

Precision — of everything a detector flagged as PII, the share that was actually PII. Recall — of all the PII present, the share the detector correctly caught. F1 — the harmonic mean of the two, a single balanced score.

Every detector is scored the same way: against all annotated PII in the benchmark, requiring an exact span match. The result is a like-for-like measure of how much real PII each one actually catches.

A note on scope. These figures come from a single public benchmark — the ai4privacy PII-Masking-300k dataset. A single dataset is a useful, repeatable yardstick, but no one dataset represents all real-world data, and a public benchmark can advantage a detector that was developed or evaluated against it. Read these results as one directional comparison, not a universal ranking.

The recall advantage

Because recall is the metric most tied to risk, it is worth viewing on its own. Skyflow catches nearly four-fifths of all PII in the benchmark — well ahead of the OpenAI Privacy Filter at roughly two-thirds, and Presidio at about a third. Every point of recall is sensitive data that would otherwise go undetected.

Takeaways

Recall is the number that matters — and Skyflow leads it. Skyflow catches ~79% of all PII, versus ~68% for the strongest alternative (the OpenAI Privacy Filter) and ~37% for Presidio. Since a single missed identifier is the real compliance and security exposure, this is the gap that most directly reduces risk.
Higher recall, with precision kept high. Skyflow also posts the best overall accuracy (0.78 F1) of any detector tested — catching markedly more PII while holding precision at 0.77, so the added coverage doesn’t come at the cost of noisy false positives.
The alternatives miss more. The OpenAI Privacy Filter, the strongest of the open-source detectors, still misses roughly a third of the PII present; Presidio’s English-first rules-and-NER approach misses closer to two-thirds and rarely detects anything beyond structured identifiers like email.

Methodology

Dataset: ai4privacy PII-Masking-300k, a 1,000-example multilingual sample (German, English, Spanish, French, Italian, Dutch). Scoring: SemEval-2013 span-level evaluation via nervaluate — every detector scored against all annotated PII with exact boundary-and-label matching.

Precision is the share of flagged spans that were real PII; recall is the share of real PII caught; F1 is their harmonic mean. Figures reflect overall results across the sample; per-language breakdowns are available. Detectors were run with default configurations and were not fine-tuned.
