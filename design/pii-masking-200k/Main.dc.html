PII Detection Accuracy & Recall

How Skyflow compares to the OpenAI Privacy Filter and Microsoft Presidio — two of the most widely used open-source PII detectors — across a broad, 15-category entity set, benchmarked on the ai4privacy PII-Masking-200k dataset.

Overview

This overview summarizes how Skyflow detects personally identifiable information (PII) across a broad range of entity types, and how its accuracy compares to two of the most widely used open-source PII detectors — the OpenAI Privacy Filter and Microsoft Presidio. All three were run over the same 1,000-example benchmark and scored the same way, so the numbers are directly comparable.

For most teams the metric that matters most is recall — the share of real PII the system actually catches. A missed identifier is a compliance and security risk, so the results below lead with recall, where Skyflow’s advantage is largest — and widest on a benchmark this diverse.

How accuracy is measured

Results use three standard measures, computed at the span level so comparisons stay consistent across detectors:

Precision — of everything a detector flagged as PII, the share that was actually PII. Recall — of all the PII present, the share the detector correctly caught. F1 — the harmonic mean of the two, a single balanced score.

Every detector is scored the same way: against all annotated PII in the benchmark, requiring an exact span match. The result is a like-for-like measure of how much real PII each one actually catches.

A note on scope. These figures come from a single public benchmark — the ai4privacy PII-Masking-200k dataset, which annotates 15 categories of PII across four languages. A single dataset is a useful, repeatable yardstick, but no one dataset represents all real-world data, and a public benchmark can advantage a detector that was developed or evaluated against it. Read these results as one directional comparison, not a universal ranking.

The recall advantage

Because recall is the metric most tied to risk, it is worth viewing on its own. Skyflow catches about two-thirds of all PII across this broad entity set — well ahead of the OpenAI Privacy Filter at roughly a third, and Presidio just under. On a benchmark this diverse the gap widens: the open-source detectors simply do not cover many of the categories present.

Takeaways

Recall is the number that matters — and Skyflow’s lead widens here. Skyflow catches ~66% of all PII across the 200k benchmark’s broad entity set, versus ~35% for the OpenAI Privacy Filter and ~29% for Presidio. On this more diverse vocabulary the gap is far larger than on narrower benchmarks — and a missed identifier is the real exposure.
Coverage is the difference. The benchmark annotates 15 categories of PII — including money, occupation, organization, vehicle, and physical descriptors. The open-source detectors model only a subset (the Privacy Filter covers eight), so they score zero on the categories they never learned. Skyflow detects across the full range in a single pass.
Broader coverage compounds into better accuracy. Skyflow posts the best precision, recall, and F1 of any detector tested (0.64 F1 versus 0.43 for the strongest alternative) — the coverage advantage carries straight through to end-to-end accuracy.

Methodology

Dataset: ai4privacy PII-Masking-200k, a 1,000-example multilingual sample (German, English, French, Italian) annotating 15 categories of PII. Scoring: SemEval-2013 span-level evaluation via nervaluate — every detector scored against all annotated PII with exact boundary-and-label matching.

Precision is the share of flagged spans that were real PII; recall is the share of real PII caught; F1 is their harmonic mean. Figures reflect overall results across the sample; per-language and per-category breakdowns are available. Detectors were run with default configurations and were not fine-tuned.
